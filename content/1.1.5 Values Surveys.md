# 📗 1.1.5 — Values Surveys

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Values surveys can be used to investigate the perspectives shown by a particular social group towards environmental issues.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

A **values survey** is a structured tool — typically a questionnaire — used to investigate the values underpinning the perspectives of a defined social group toward a specific environmental issue, and to assess how those values are likely to shape the group's response to that issue.

Questionnaires can use three main question types:
- **Closed questions** — fixed-choice answers, easy to analyse statistically
- **Open questions** — free-text responses, giving richer but harder-to-compare detail
- **Scale questions** — rating agreement or ranking importance

A well-designed instrument is short, clearly worded, logically sequenced, avoids overly personal questions, and is **piloted** before full use to catch ambiguous wording.

**Interviews** are a related but distinct method: they allow more depth and follow-up than a questionnaire but are more time-consuming and are therefore usually conducted with a much smaller sample. Both tools can be visualised using devices such as pie charts or **behaviour-over-time graphs** (BoTGs), which track how a group's environmental attitudes change across years.

## ⚖️ Evaluation

Values surveys allow systematic comparison of perspectives across different social groups, making patterns visible that might otherwise be hidden. However, results can be distorted by **response bias** — respondents answering in a way they believe is socially desirable rather than truthfully. Sample size and representativeness also affect how far results can be generalised. Interviews address some of these limitations through follow-up questions, but their small sample sizes limit statistical power.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Values survey | A research tool, typically a questionnaire, used to investigate the values underpinning a social group's perspectives on an issue. |
| Closed question | A survey question with a fixed set of possible answers, useful for statistical analysis. |
| Open question | A survey question allowing a free-text response, providing more detailed but less easily comparable data. |
| Scale question | A survey question asking respondents to rate their level of agreement or rank items in order of importance. |
| Response bias | A tendency for survey respondents to answer in a way they believe is socially desirable rather than truthfully. |
| Pilot study | A small-scale trial of a research instrument (e.g. questionnaire) carried out before the main study to identify and correct problems. |
| Behaviour-over-time graph (BoTG) | A line graph plotting the change in a factor across time, used to visualise trends such as shifting attitudes. |
