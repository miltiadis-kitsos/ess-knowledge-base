# 📗 1.1.4 — How Values Are Shown

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *1.1.4  The values that underpin our perspectives can be seen in our communication and actions with the wider community. The values held by organizations can be seen through advertisements, media, policies and actions.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

Values are not just internal; they become visible through what **individuals** and **organisations** communicate and do. An individual's values might show up in everyday choices — how they vote, what they buy, what causes they support publicly — while an organisation's values are typically visible through more formal channels such as advertisements, media coverage, official policies and concrete actions.

Crucially, there can be a gap between an organisation's *stated* policy and its *actual* actions. Because of this gap, actions are considered stronger evidence of an organisation's true values than words or public relations campaigns alone.

## 🔍 Case Studies

**Volkswagen "Clean Diesel" Scandal (2015) — Greenwashing**

VW's corporate policy and PR campaigns branded its diesel engines as environmentally friendly "clean diesel," while secretly installing defeat-device software that cheated emissions tests. When the US EPA exposed the fraud in September 2015, roughly 11 million vehicles worldwide were found to be affected, costing VW over $30 billion in fines and settlements and costing its CEO his job.

The scandal harmed consumers who had made purchasing decisions based on false environmental claims, shareholders who lost significant value, and regulators whose trust in manufacturer self-reporting was undermined — making it a textbook example of the gap between stated policy and actual actions.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The VW case illustrates why external scrutiny and independent verification are essential: self-reported values (via advertising or policy documents) are easy to manipulate, while observable actions — especially when independently tested — are much harder to fake. However, not all gaps between stated values and actions are deliberate deception; institutional inertia, poor governance, or conflicting priorities within large organisations can also produce inconsistency.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Greenwashing | The practice of an organisation presenting itself as more environmentally responsible than its actions justify. |
| Corporate policy | A formal, documented set of principles or rules that guides an organisation's decisions and conduct. |
| Stakeholder | A person or group with an interest in, or affected by, the actions of an individual or organisation. |
| Public relations (PR) | The managed communication activities an organisation uses to shape public perception of itself. |
| Consumer choice | The purchasing decisions individuals make, which can reflect and communicate their personal values. |
