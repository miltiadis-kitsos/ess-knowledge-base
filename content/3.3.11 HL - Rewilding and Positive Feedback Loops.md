# 📗 3.3.11 — Rewilding and Positive Feedback Loops

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Positive feedback loops that enhance biodiversity and promote ecosystem equilibrium can be triggered by rewilding and habitat restoration efforts.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

A **positive feedback loop** amplifies a change — in an ecological context, this means an initial restoration action triggers a cascade of improvements that reinforce each other, driving the ecosystem toward a new, more biodiverse **alternative stable state**.

Key ideas:
- Positive feedback is a **necessary condition** for the emergence of alternative stable states at the community scale.
- Enhanced growth and biomass, reproduction, and survival of species all influence food web interactions.
- **Rewilding** — reintroducing species or reducing human intervention — can trigger these loops.

---

## 📊 Comparisons / Tables

| Stage | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Reintroduction of an apex predator (e.g. grey wolf) |
| **Trophic effect** | Reduced overgrazing by prey species (elk, deer) |
| **Vegetation recovery** | Saplings flourish; root systems stabilise riverbanks |
| **Engineer species return** | Beavers and others reshape habitat structure |
| **Biodiversity increase** | More niches available; species richness rises |
| **New stable state** | System stabilises at a higher biodiversity equilibrium |

---

## 🔍 Case Studies

### Grey Wolf Reintroduction — Yellowstone National Park, USA

**The trigger (Rewilding):** Grey wolves, extirpated for ~70 years, were reintroduced to Yellowstone in 1995.

**The positive feedback cascade:**
1. Growing wolf population preyed on elk and deer; controlled coyote numbers.
2. Herbivores became more cautious → reduced overgrazing.
3. Young saplings (willow, aspen) flourished → increased plant biomass.
4. Roots stabilised riverbanks → reduced erosion.
5. Beavers returned as "ecosystem engineers" → created ponds and wetland habitat.
6. Stabilised banks and new habitats attracted badgers, eagles, foxes, and songbirds.
7. Wolf carrion provided a year-round food source for scavengers and other carnivores.
8. **Outcome:** Complex food web interactions created a new alternative stable state — a more biodiverse, resilient ecosystem.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The Yellowstone case demonstrates that **one targeted intervention** (reintroducing an apex predator) can trigger a self-reinforcing cascade of ecological improvements. However, this depends on:
- Sufficient habitat size and connectivity.
- Absence of competing human land uses.
- Long-term protection of the reintroduced species.

Not all rewilding efforts produce such dramatic results — success depends heavily on the starting conditions and the complexity of the existing food web.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Positive feedback loop | A process where an initial change triggers further changes in the same direction, amplifying the effect |
| Alternative stable state | A different equilibrium condition an ecosystem can settle into after a major disturbance or intervention |
| Rewilding | The large-scale restoration of ecosystems by reintroducing species or removing human pressures |
| Trophic cascade | Indirect effects on lower trophic levels caused by changes in the abundance of a top predator |
| Ecosystem engineer | A species that significantly modifies its habitat, creating conditions for other species (e.g. beavers) |
| Apex predator | A predator at the top of the food chain with no natural predators |
