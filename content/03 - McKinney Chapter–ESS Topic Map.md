# 📘 McKinney — Chapter to IB ESS Topic Map

**Book:** *Environmental Science: Systems and Solutions*, 5th Edition (2013)  
**Authors:** McKinney, Schoch, Yonavjak — Jones & Bartlett Learning  
**Syllabus:** IB ESS 2025+ (First Assessments 2025)

---

## 📌 How to Use This Note

Use the table below to find which McKinney chapters support each IB ESS topic when planning lessons, building notes, or setting supplementary reading. Compare with [[02 - Cunningham Chapter–ESS Topic Map]] to find the strongest source for any given topic.

---

## 📊 Chapter → ESS Topic Map

### Section 1 — Introduction

| # | Chapter Title | IB ESS Topic(s) | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| Intro | A Guide to the Environment | — | Overview of environmental problems and systems thinking; good entry point for T1 |
| 1 | Environmental Science: An Overview | **T1** Foundations of ESS | History of environmentalism, sustainability, ecological footprint, environmental worldviews (EVS) |
| 2 | Human Population Growth | **T8** Human Systems & Resource Use | Demographic transition, age structures, population policies, urbanisation trends |

### Section 2 — The Biosphere

| # | Chapter Title | IB ESS Topic(s) | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| 3 | The Biosphere: Populations, Communities, Ecosystems & Biogeochemical Cycles | **T2** Ecosystems | Energy flow, food webs, trophic levels, C/N/P/H₂O cycles — dense, systems-rich chapter |
| 4 | The Distribution of Life on Earth | **T2** Ecosystems | Biomes, aquatic & terrestrial ecosystems, biodiversity patterns — strong link to ESS biome content |
| 5 | The Dynamic Earth & Natural Hazards | **T1** Foundations of ESS · **T8** Human Systems | Geological processes, plate tectonics, natural disasters — systems framing; risk & vulnerability |

### Section 3 — Resource Use and Management

| # | Chapter Title | IB ESS Topic(s) | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| 6 | People and Natural Resources | **T8** Human Systems & Resource Use | Resource depletion, tragedy of the commons, ecological footprint, sustainability frameworks |
| 7 | Fundamentals of Energy, Fossil Fuels & Nuclear Energy | **T7** Energy, Industry & Society | EROI, fossil fuel extraction & impacts, nuclear fission/fusion, energy security |
| 8 | Renewable & Alternative Energy Sources | **T7** Energy, Industry & Society | Solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, bioenergy — technologies, limitations, storage challenges |
| 9 | Water Resources | **T4** Water Systems | Global freshwater distribution, dams, irrigation, groundwater, water governance, scarcity |
| 10 | Mineral Resources | **T8** Human Systems & Resource Use | Mining impacts, strategic minerals, recycling, circular economy, e-waste |
| 11 | Conserving Biological Resources | **T3** Biodiversity & Conservation | Biodiversity value, extinction drivers, protected areas, in-situ & ex-situ conservation, CITES |
| 12 | Land Resources and Management | **T3** Biodiversity & Conservation · **T5** Soil | Deforestation, wetland loss, habitat fragmentation, land use planning, protected landscapes |
| 13 | Food and Soil Resources | **T5** Soil & Terrestrial Food Production | Soil degradation, erosion, salinisation, Green Revolution, organic farming, food security |

### Section 4 — Dealing with Environmental Degradation

| # | Chapter Title | IB ESS Topic(s) | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| 14 | Air Pollution: Local and Regional | **T6** Atmosphere & Climate | Primary/secondary pollutants, smog, acid rain, indoor air quality, health impacts |
| 15 | Global Air Pollution: Ozone Depletion & Climate Change | **T6** Atmosphere & Climate | Greenhouse effect, IPCC, climate feedbacks, ozone layer — strongest climate chapter in this book |
| 16 | Water Pollution | **T4** Water Systems | Point vs non-point sources, eutrophication, sewage treatment, groundwater contamination, ocean plastic |
| 17 | Municipal Solid Waste & Hazardous Waste | **T8** Human Systems & Resource Use | Landfill, incineration, recycling, e-waste, toxic substances, risk assessment |

### Section 5 — Social Solutions

| # | Chapter Title | IB ESS Topic(s) | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| 18 | Economics, Politics & Environmental Policy | **T1** Foundations of ESS · **T8** Human Systems | Natural capital, externalities, cost-benefit analysis, MEAs, international governance, EIA |
| 19 | Toward a Sustainable Future | **T1** Foundations of ESS | Environmental worldviews, personal action, green economy, sustainable development — excellent for EVS |

## 📋 Comparing McKinney vs Cunningham for ESS Teaching

| IB ESS Topic | Stronger Source | Notes |
|--------------|-----------------|-------|
| T1 Foundations / EVS | **Cunningham** Ch 1, 23, 25 | More nuanced on values, SDGs, planetary boundaries |
| T2 Ecosystems | **McKinney** Ch 3, 4 | More systems-focused; biogeochemical cycles in one dense chapter |
| T3 Biodiversity | **Cunningham** Ch 11–13 | Three dedicated chapters vs McKinney's two |
| T4 Water | **Equal** | Cunningham splits use/pollution (Ch 17–18); McKinney integrates (Ch 9, 16) |
| T5 Soil & Food | **Cunningham** Ch 10 | More detailed on sustainable farming practices |
| T6 Atmosphere & Climate | **Cunningham** Ch 15–16 | More up to date (2024 vs 2013) |
| T7 Energy | **Equal** | Both devote two chapters; Cunningham more current |
| T8 Human Systems | **McKinney** Ch 2, 6, 18 | Stronger economics and policy framing |
