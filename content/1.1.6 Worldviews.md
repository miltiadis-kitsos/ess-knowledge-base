# 📗 1.1.6 — Worldviews

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Worldviews are the lenses shared by groups of people through which they perceive, make sense of and act within their environment. They shape people's values and perspectives through culture, philosophy, ideology, religion and politics.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

A **worldview** differs from a **perspective** in scale: a perspective belongs to an individual, while a worldview is a lens *shared across a group*, shaped collectively by culture, philosophy, ideology, religion and politics. Worldviews in turn shape the values and perspectives of the individuals within that group.

Comparing religious worldviews illustrates how differently the human–environment relationship can be framed:
- **Christian traditions** often frame humans as *stewards*, given responsibility for the Earth by a higher power.
- **Buddhist traditions** tend to frame humans as an intrinsic *part of nature* rather than separate from or responsible for it, emphasising interdependence over stewardship.

## 🔍 Case Studies

**Buddhist Monks and Forest Conservation — Thailand**

Buddhist monks in Thailand have become active participants in ecological conservation, including forest-conservation projects, and have worked to teach ecologically sound farming practices to local communities. This was a direct response to environmental degradation linked to rapid, unsustainable economic development.

Because Buddhist monks are held in high social respect in Thailand, their environmental advocacy carries significant influence and has helped shift local attitudes and farming practices toward more sustainable approaches. This case shows a worldview (Buddhist interdependence with nature) translating directly into collective action and measurable influence on a society's environmental behaviour.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

Worldviews are powerful explanatory tools because they reveal *why* different groups respond so differently to the same environmental problem. However, worldviews are not monolithic — considerable variation exists within any religion or culture, and worldviews evolve over time. Treating worldviews as fixed categories risks oversimplification and can obscure the agency of individuals within a group.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Worldview | The lens shared by a group of people through which they perceive, make sense of, and act within, their environment. |
| Perspective | An individual's particular attitude toward or way of regarding an issue, shaped by their values and worldview. |
| Stewardship | A worldview in which humans are given responsibility for managing and caring for the Earth's resources on behalf of, or under the authority of, a higher power. |
| Interdependence | A worldview emphasising the interconnectedness of humans and the rest of the natural world, rather than human separateness from it. |
| Culture | The shared beliefs, values, customs, and practices of a group of people, transmitted across generations. |
