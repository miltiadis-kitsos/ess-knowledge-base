# 📗 4.1.3 — Major Water Stores

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Identify the relative sizes of the main stores in the hydrological cycle.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

Water on Earth is distributed very unequally across a range of stores. The vast majority of Earth's water is saline and stored in the oceans, making freshwater a scarce and unevenly distributed resource. Understanding the relative sizes of these stores is fundamental to appreciating why freshwater availability is a critical global challenge.

The most important distinction to understand is that **approximately 97% of all water on Earth is saline** (found in the oceans), while only **3% is freshwater**. Of that freshwater, the majority is locked in glaciers and ice caps, meaning liquid freshwater that is accessible to humans and ecosystems represents a tiny fraction of Earth's total water.

---

## 📊 Comparisons / Tables

| Store | % of Total Water | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Oceans** | 96.5% | Primary reservoir; saline |
| **Glaciers and Ice Caps** | 1.7% | Frozen freshwater; inaccessible without melting |
| **Groundwater** | 1.7% | Mostly freshwater; slow to recharge |
| **Surface Freshwater** (lakes, rivers, swamps) | 0.02% | Most accessible store for humans and ecosystems |
| **Atmosphere** | 0.001% | Water vapour; short residence time |
| **Organisms** | 0.0001% | Water within living biomass |

**Key figures to remember:**
- ~**97%** of Earth's water is saline
- ~**3%** is freshwater
- Most freshwater is **frozen** (glaciers/ice caps), not liquid

---

## 🔍 Case Studies

The scarcity of accessible freshwater is central to understanding water security crises:
- **Aral Sea (4.1.5):** Unsustainable use of surface freshwater stores (river inflows) for irrigation caused catastrophic shrinkage of the sea — demonstrating how fragile surface freshwater stores are.
- **Groundwater depletion:** Many regions (e.g. the Ogallala Aquifer in the USA) are extracting groundwater faster than it is recharged, effectively drawing down a store with a residence time of thousands of years.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The distribution data makes clear that accessible freshwater is extraordinarily limited — surface freshwater (lakes, rivers) represents just 0.02% of total water. This scarcity is further complicated by the uneven global distribution of freshwater stores: glaciers are concentrated in polar and high-altitude regions, while groundwater recharge rates vary enormously. As climate change melts glaciers and alters precipitation patterns, the sizes and locations of these stores will shift, with profound implications for human water security.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Ocean | The largest store of water on Earth (~96.5%); primarily saline |
| Glacier | A large, slow-moving mass of ice; stores ~1.7% of Earth's water as frozen freshwater |
| Groundwater | Water stored in underground aquifers; accounts for ~1.7% of total water |
| Surface freshwater | Liquid freshwater in lakes, rivers, and swamps; only ~0.02% of total water |
| Saline water | Water containing dissolved salts; makes up ~97% of all water on Earth |
| Aquifer | An underground layer of permeable rock or sediment that holds groundwater |
