## 🔑 Key Cross-Links

**Driving forces → Types of flow:**
[[4.1.1 - Driving Forces of the Hydrosphere]] explains *why* water moves (solar energy, gravity); [[4.1.4 - Transfers and Transformations]] classifies *how* it moves (transfer vs. transformation). Evaporation = transformation driven by solar energy; river flow = transfer driven by gravity.

**Hydrological cycle → Steady state:**
[[4.1.2 - The Hydrological Cycle as a System]] introduces the conceptual model of stores and flows; [[4.1.6 - Steady State and Water Body Modelling]] applies it quantitatively to a single water body (lake/reservoir). The Aral Sea (4.1.5) is the most powerful example of that steady state being destroyed.

**Human impacts → Systems disruption:**
[[4.1.5 - Human Impact on Water Systems]] should always be read in light of [[1.2.8-1.2.9 - Negative Feedback and Equilibrium]] — human activities overwhelm the natural negative feedbacks that normally keep water stores in balance. The Aral Sea is a tipping point example (1.2.10–1.2.12) in a water context.

**Water stores → Freshwater scarcity:**
[[4.1.3 - Major Water Stores]] establishes *why* freshwater is scarce (most is locked in ice); [[4.1.5 - Human Impact on Water Systems]] shows the additional pressure humans put on what little accessible freshwater exists. Together they motivate the entire "resource security" framing of Unit 4.

**Unique properties → Aquatic life and chemistry (HL):**
[[4.1.7 - Unique Properties of Water]] (gas solubility — CO₂ dissolves in water) is the physical foundation for [[4.1.8 HL - Oceans as a Carbon Sink]] — oceans absorb CO₂ precisely because of water's solvent properties. The density anomaly also relates to [[4.1.9 HL - Ocean Stratification]] — stratification exists because warmer water is less dense.

**Stratification ↔ Thermohaline circulation:**
[[4.1.9 HL - Ocean Stratification]] (layers resist mixing → nutrients trapped at depth) and [[4.1.10 HL - Thermohaline Circulation and Upwellings]] (deep cold water eventually rises, delivering nutrients) are opposing forces — THC partially overcomes stratification in key upwelling zones, creating the world's most productive fisheries.

## 📝 Key Figures & Case Studies at a Glance

| Case Study | Location | Key Point | Note |
|-----------|---------|-----------|------|
| Aral Sea collapse | Kazakhstan/Uzbekistan | Irrigation (cotton) diverted feeder rivers → sea shrank to <10% original volume → salt desert; ~60,000 fishing jobs lost | [[4.1.5 - Human Impact on Water Systems]] |
| Amazon deforestation → water cycle | Brazil | Deforestation reduces transpiration → less atmospheric moisture → reduced rainfall → further forest die-back (positive feedback) | [[4.1.5 - Human Impact on Water Systems]] |
| London SuDS / urban flooding | London, UK | Impermeable surfaces → flashy hydrographs; Sustainable Urban Drainage Systems (SuDS) restore infiltration | [[4.1.5 - Human Impact on Water Systems]] |
| Bromeliad micro-ecosystem | Costa Rica | Complete water cycle in miniature — storage, evaporation, rainfall input, overflow | [[4.1.7 - Unique Properties of Water]] & [[1.2.7 - Systems at a Range of Scales]] |
| Ocean acidification | Global oceans | pH dropped from ~8.2 to ~8.1 since Industrial Revolution = 26% increase in acidity; threatens calcifying organisms | [[4.1.8 HL - Oceans as a Carbon Sink]] |
| Biological pump | Global oceans | Phytoplankton photosynthesis → dead biomass sinks → millions of years of carbon burial → fossil fuels | [[4.1.8 HL - Oceans as a Carbon Sink]] |
| ENSO / El Niño | Pacific Ocean | Trade winds weaken → warm water moves east → upwelling stops → Peru/Ecuador fisheries collapse | [[4.1.10 HL - Thermohaline Circulation and Upwellings]] |
