# 📗 1.2.5 — Open and Closed Systems

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *1.2.5 Systems can be open or closed.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

An **open system** exchanges both matter and energy across its boundary with the surrounding environment. All living organisms and all ecosystems are open systems: they must take in energy and matter, and produce waste.

A **closed system** exchanges energy but not matter across its boundary. Matter is recycled internally within the system. An **isolated system** exchanges neither energy nor matter across its boundary.

Closed systems are extremely rare in nature. The Earth approximates a closed system: solar radiation (energy) enters and heat energy is radiated to space, but matter (other than the occasional meteorite or spacecraft) does not cross the boundary. Global biogeochemical cycles (water, carbon, nitrogen) also approximate closed systems at the planetary scale. Most practical closed systems are artificial and constructed for experimental purposes (e.g. a sealed terrarium, Biosphere 2).

| System type | Energy exchanged? | Matter exchanged? | Example |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|---------|
| Open | Yes | Yes | Ecosystem; human body |
| Closed | Yes | No | Biosphere 2; the Earth (approximately) |
| Isolated | No | No | Theoretical only |

---

## 📊 Diagrams / Tables

The distinction matters because open systems depend on external inputs and produce outputs that affect other systems — making them far harder to manage sustainably than closed systems. Nutrient cycles in ecosystems approximate closed systems (matter is recycled), while energy flow is open (solar energy in, heat out).

## 🔍 Case Studies

**Biosphere 2, Oracle, Arizona, USA (1987–1993)**

![[topic12_img15.jpeg]]

Biosphere 2 was a sealed, 3-hectare greenhouse constructed in Arizona as a prototype of a closed, self-sustaining biosphere — modelling the Earth and exploring the feasibility of closed systems for space colonisation. It housed five biomes (tropical rainforest, coral reef ocean, mangrove wetlands, savanna and fog desert) plus an agricultural zone and crew quarters. Eight scientists were sealed inside for two years (1991–1993).

Critical failures emerged: atmospheric O₂ fell from 21% to 14% (equivalent to living at 4,000 m altitude) — traced to overactive soil microbes consuming oxygen; 19 of 25 small vertebrate species went extinct; ants, cockroaches and katydids overran the system; crop production was insufficient to feed the crew adequately.

The experiment demonstrated that even a technologically advanced closed system quickly becomes unstable because the complexity of real ecosystem interactions — including microbial communities and feedback loops — is not fully understood or predictable. *Note: Biosphere 2 was never truly a closed system; energy entered as electricity from natural gas, and O₂ was augmented when levels became dangerous, making it partially open in practice.*

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The open/closed distinction is conceptually useful but rarely absolute in real systems. Calling the Earth a "closed system" is an approximation — small amounts of matter are gained (meteorites) and lost (hydrogen escaping from the atmosphere). More importantly, the distinction highlights a crucial point for ESS: because ecosystems are open systems, they are constantly dependent on energy and matter inputs from outside. Disrupting those flows (e.g. blocking sunlight through particulate pollution, or cutting off nutrient inputs to a wetland) can rapidly destabilise a seemingly stable system. The Biosphere 2 case also shows that our ability to engineer closed systems is far more limited than we assume.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Open system | A system that exchanges both matter and energy with its surrounding environment across its boundary. |
| Closed system | A system that exchanges energy but not matter with its surrounding environment; matter is recycled internally. |
| Isolated system | A theoretical system that exchanges neither energy nor matter with its environment. |
| Biosphere 2 | An artificial 3-hectare sealed biosphere in Arizona (USA) constructed to model the Earth as a closed system; its failures demonstrated the complexity of sustainable closed ecosystems. |
| Biogeochemical cycle | A pathway by which a chemical element (e.g. carbon, nitrogen) moves through the biotic and abiotic components of an ecosystem; approximates a closed system at the planetary scale. |
