# 📗 1.1.7 — Environmental Value Systems (EVS)

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *An environmental value system is a model that shows the inputs affecting our perspectives and the outputs resulting from our perspectives.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

An **Environmental Value System (EVS)** is a model that uses systems thinking to explain how an individual's values and perspectives are formed and expressed. Like any system, an EVS has **inputs**, **processes** and **outputs**:

- **Inputs** — the influences that shape an individual's perspectives: education, worldviews, media, culture, economic factors, sociopolitical factors, and religious doctrine.
- **Process** — the individual's internal processing of these inputs through their existing values and worldview.
- **Outputs** — the judgements, positions, choices and courses of action that result: what the individual believes, says and does about environmental issues.

![[eSS as a system.png]]

EVSs operate within broader **social systems**, which — like ecosystems — exchange information, ideas and people rather than matter and energy, but share structural features with ecosystems such as feedback and equilibrium. A change in one input (e.g. a major environmental disaster) can shift outputs (e.g. public policy, consumer behaviour) through feedback loops within the social system.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The EVS model is a useful simplification that makes the complexity of perspective-formation tractable. It highlights that no perspective appears from nowhere — every viewpoint has identifiable causes. However, the model has limits: it treats the individual as a relatively passive processor of inputs, underplaying agency and the capacity for critical reflection. In practice, people can consciously interrogate and revise their own values, which the input-output diagram does not fully capture.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Environmental value system (EVS) | A model showing the inputs affecting our perspectives and the outputs resulting from our perspectives. |
| Input (to a system) | A factor or resource entering a system — in this context, the influences that shape an individual's EVS. |
| Output (of a system) | The result produced by a system after processing its inputs — in this context, the judgements, positions, choices and actions arising from an EVS. |
| Sociopolitical factors | The combined influence of social and political conditions (e.g. governance style) on perspectives. |
| Social system | A system involving flows of information, ideas and people rather than matter and energy. |
| Feedback | A process by which the output of a system is fed back as an input, influencing subsequent outputs. |
