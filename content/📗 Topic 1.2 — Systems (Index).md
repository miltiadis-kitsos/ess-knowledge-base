## 🔑 Key Cross-Links

**Systems diagrams → Transfers & Transformations:**
[[1.2.1-1.2.3 - What Is a System and System Diagrams]] introduces flows as arrows; [[1.2.4 - Transfers and Transformations]] distinguishes *what kind* of flow each arrow represents — essential for labelling diagrams correctly.

**Open/Closed → Earth as a system:**
[[1.2.5 - Open and Closed Systems]] establishes that the Earth approximates a closed system for matter; [[1.2.6 - The Earth as a Single Integrated System]] then explains *how* the six spheres interact within that closed matter budget — and what happens when the anthroposphere disrupts those interactions.

**Scale → Gaia:**
[[1.2.7 - Systems at a Range of Scales]] (bromeliad to biosphere) leads naturally to [[1.2.6 - The Earth as a Single Integrated System]] (the Gaia hypothesis as a global-scale systems argument). The same feedback logic operates at every scale.

**Negative ↔ Positive feedback:**
[[1.2.8-1.2.9 - Negative Feedback and Equilibrium]] and [[1.2.10-1.2.12 - Positive Feedback and Tipping Points]] must always be taught and revised together — they are opposites. The lynx–hare system *stays* at equilibrium; the Amazon risks *crossing* a tipping point. The difference is whether the feedback counteracts or amplifies.

**Models → Everything:**
[[1.2.13-1.2.14 - Models]] closes the topic by explaining the tools used to represent all the systems described above. The Biosphere 2 case study (physical model, from 1.2.5) reappears here; DaisyWorld (from 1.2.6) is an example of a mathematical model.

## 📝 Key Figures & Case Studies at a Glance

| Biosphere 2 (Arizona, USA, 1991) | Physical model / closed system | Closed system failure — O₂ crashed, species died; complexity of real ecosystems underestimated | [[1.2.5 - Open and Closed Systems]] & [[1.2.13-1.2.14 - Models]] |
| Snowshoe Hare & Canadian Lynx (Hudson Bay, 1850–1925) | Negative feedback / dynamic equilibrium | ~10-year population cycle; time lags cause oscillation not smooth return | [[1.2.8-1.2.9 - Negative Feedback and Equilibrium]] |

| Permafrost–carbon feedback | Positive feedback | Warming → CH₄/CO₂ release → further warming | [[1.2.10-1.2.12 - Positive Feedback and Tipping Points]] |
