# 📗 3.3.14 — Ecotourism: Benefits and Impacts

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Ecotourism can increase interdependence of local communities and increase biodiversity by generating income and providing funds for protecting areas, but there can also be negative societal and ecological impacts.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

**Ecotourism** is tourism directed toward natural environments, intended to support conservation and local communities while minimising ecological impact. It creates a direct financial link between biodiversity and local livelihoods — giving communities an economic incentive to protect, rather than exploit, natural resources.

Key concept: **Interdependence** — when local communities depend on a healthy ecosystem for their income, they become active stakeholders in its conservation.

---

## 📊 Comparisons / Tables

| | Positive Impacts | Negative Impacts |
|---|---|---|
| **Ecological** | Direct funding for habitat restoration and species protection | Biosecurity risks (invasive species introduced via transport); habitat disturbance |
| **Social** | Provides livelihoods; financial incentive to prevent poaching | Displacement of indigenous communities; "commodification" of local culture |
| **Economic** | Diversifies local income; funds conservation bodies | Over-dependence on a volatile industry (e.g. pandemic shut-downs) |
| **Educational** | Raises global awareness; tourists become conservation "ambassadors" | Pollution and waste management strain on fragile ecosystems |

---

## 🔍 Case Studies

### Galápagos Islands, Ecuador

**Context:** An archipelago renowned as a "living laboratory" of evolution, home to iconic endemic species (Giant Tortoises, Marine Iguanas, Blue-footed Boobies). A premier ecotourism destination.

---

#### ✅ Positive Impacts

**Funding for conservation:**
- International tourist entrance fees (**$200+ per visitor**) go directly to the Galápagos National Park and the Charles Darwin Foundation.
- Funds invasive species eradication programmes (goats, rats) and Giant Tortoise protection.

**Economic interdependence:**
- Ecotourism provides livelihoods for **over 80% of the local population**.
- Community financial dependency on the environment creates strong incentives to prevent illegal fishing and poaching.

**Education and awareness:**
- All tours are led by certified naturalist guides, ensuring visitors understand the ecological significance of what they observe.
- Tourists often leave as advocates for conservation — the "ambassador effect."

---

#### ❌ Negative Impacts

**Invasive species introduction:**
- Increased air and sea traffic has accidentally introduced species including **West Nile Virus**, **fire ants**, and **invasive blackberries**, which threaten endemic wildlife.

**Resource pressure and pollution:**
- The surge in visitors (and the workforce to serve them) strains the islands' freshwater supply, generates sewage, and creates solid waste that can enter the marine ecosystem.

**Societal "gentrification":**
- High tourism revenue drives up cost of living for locals not directly employed in tourism.
- Local traditions risk being "commodified" — performed for profit rather than maintained as living culture.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

The Galápagos illustrates ecotourism's central paradox: the very appeal that draws visitors can be degraded by the act of visiting. When well-managed, ecotourism is one of the most powerful tools for aligning community and conservation interests. When poorly regulated, it accelerates the ecological and cultural damage it was meant to prevent. **Strict visitor caps, biosecurity protocols, and revenue reinvestment** into conservation are essential safeguards.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Ecotourism | Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the wellbeing of local people |
| Interdependence | A relationship where communities and ecosystems mutually depend on each other for wellbeing and survival |
| Biosecurity risk | The danger of introducing harmful organisms (pathogens, invasive species) through increased human movement |
| Commodification | The process of treating cultural practices or natural features as commercial products |
| Ambassador effect | The phenomenon where tourists educated about an environment become advocates for its protection |
| Invasive species | A non-native species whose introduction causes ecological or economic harm |
