## 📖 Key Concepts

**Thermohaline circulation** (THC) is a global system of ocean currents driven by differences in water **temperature** (thermo) and **salinity** (haline), which together determine water **density**. It is often described as the "Global Ocean Conveyor Belt" because it transports water — and with it, heat, nutrients, and dissolved gases — continuously around the planet.

**How it works:**
In the North Atlantic, warm surface water from the tropics (carried by the Gulf Stream) cools as it moves northward. Cooling increases density. At the same time, evaporation concentrates salt in the water (increasing salinity further increases density). This dense water sinks to the deep ocean in a process called **deep water formation**. It then flows southward along the ocean floor as a deep current, eventually rising (upwelling) in other ocean basins before returning as warm surface water. The complete circuit takes approximately **1,000–2,000 years**.

**Climate regulation:**
THC plays a critical role in regulating global climate by redistributing heat from equatorial regions toward the poles. The Gulf Stream, for example, keeps Western Europe significantly warmer than other regions at similar latitudes (e.g. Canada). Disruption of THC — particularly through freshwater influx from melting ice sheets, which reduces surface salinity and inhibits sinking — is one of the most concerning potential consequences of climate change.

**Upwellings:**
**Upwellings** are localised events where cold, nutrient-rich deep water rises to the surface. They occur along coastlines where prevailing winds push surface water offshore (Ekman transport), drawing cold water upward to replace it. Upwellings dramatically increase marine productivity by bringing nutrients into the photic zone.

**Link to ENSO:**
Both THC and upwellings are strongly influenced by the **El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO)**. During **El Niño**, trade winds weaken or reverse, reducing upwelling along the South American coast (e.g. the Humboldt Current), collapsing fishery productivity and altering global precipitation patterns. During **La Niña**, stronger trade winds enhance upwelling and productivity.

## 🔍 Case Studies

**Humboldt Current Upwelling — Peru and Chile:**
The Humboldt Current brings cold, nutrient-rich water to the surface along the Pacific coast of South America, supporting one of the world's most productive fisheries (anchovy, sardine). During **El Niño** years, weakening trade winds suppress this upwelling, causing warm water to spread eastward, dramatically reducing fish catches and causing ecological and economic disruption across the region.

**Gulf Stream and European Climate:**
The Gulf Stream (part of THC) transports warm tropical water northward to the North Atlantic, keeping Western Europe 5–10°C warmer than it would otherwise be. Climate models suggest that melting Greenland ice could inject large volumes of freshwater into the North Atlantic, diluting salinity, reducing surface water density, and potentially weakening or halting deep water formation — slowing the conveyor belt and dramatically altering European climate.

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

Thermohaline circulation is one of Earth's most important climate stabilisers, and its potential disruption under climate change represents one of the most significant and difficult-to-reverse risks in the Earth system. Upwellings, though more localised, are vital for global fisheries and food security. Both systems highlight the deep interdependence between ocean physics, climate, and human wellbeing — and both are vulnerable to the same underlying driver: climate change altering the temperature and salinity gradients that sustain them.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Thermohaline circulation (THC) | Global ocean circulation driven by density differences caused by temperature and salinity |
| Conveyor belt | Informal name for THC — highlights its role in continuously circulating water around all ocean basins |
| Deep water formation | The sinking of cold, dense, saline surface water in polar regions, driving THC |
| Upwelling | The vertical movement of cold, nutrient-rich deep water to the surface, driven by wind |
| ENSO | El Niño Southern Oscillation — a periodic climate pattern affecting trade winds and ocean temperatures |
| El Niño | A phase of ENSO where weakened trade winds reduce upwelling and warm the Eastern Pacific |
| Ekman transport | Wind-driven movement of surface water perpendicular to the wind direction, causing upwelling |
| Halocline | A layer of ocean water with a rapid change in salinity with depth (analogous to thermocline for temperature) |
