# 📗 3.3.10 — Conservation Organisations and Success Factors

---

## 🎯 Syllabus Statement

> *Success in conserving and restoring biodiversity by international, governmental and non-governmental organizations depends on their use of media, speed of response, diplomatic constraints, financial resources and political influence.*

---

## 📖 Key Concepts

The success of any conservation effort depends on the **type of organisation** involved and the resources and strategies available to it. Three main types exist:

- **IGOs (Intergovernmental Organisations)** — e.g. UNEP. Formed by agreements between nations.
- **GOs (Governmental Organisations)** — e.g. US EPA. National-level bodies with legal authority.
- **NGOs (Non-Governmental Organisations)** — e.g. Greenpeace. Independent, often publicly funded.

Each type differs in four critical dimensions: media use, speed of response, diplomatic constraints, and enforcement power.

---

## 📊 Comparisons / Tables

| Feature | IGOs / GOs (e.g. UNEP, EPA) | NGOs (e.g. Greenpeace) |
|---|---|---|
| **Media Use** | Conservative; official statements via controlled media liaison. | Radical; high-profile stunts and protest footage to gain public attention. |
| **Response Speed** | **Slow** — hindered by bureaucracy and need for diplomatic consensus. | **Fast** — shared internal consensus; no bureaucratic "red tape." |
| **Diplomatic Constraints** | **High** — must navigate political disagreements and international treaties. | **Low** — can take idealistic or uncompromising stances; not bound by politics. |
| **Enforcement** | Legal power to create international treaties (IGOs) or national laws (GOs). | No legal power; acts as a "watchdog" relying on public persuasion. |

---

## 🔍 Case Studies

**NGO — Greenpeace**
- Mobilises public action directly "in the field."
- Financially dependent on fluctuating public donations, limiting long-term stability.

**IGO — UNEP (UN Environment Programme)**
- Significant global influence — e.g. the Montreal Protocol on ozone-depleting substances.
- Hampered by the need for consensus among many nations with conflicting interests.

**GO — US EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)**
- Has direct power to enforce environmental legislation at the national level.
- Priorities are vulnerable to shifts in national political climate (e.g. change of administration).

---

## ⚖️ Evaluation

IGOs and GOs carry greater **legitimacy and enforcement power**, but their slow, consensus-driven processes can make them ineffective in rapidly unfolding crises. NGOs are **agile and media-savvy** but lack legal authority and sustainable funding. The most effective conservation outcomes tend to emerge when IGOs/GOs set the legal framework and NGOs apply pressure and public awareness.

## 🧠 Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| IGO | Intergovernmental Organisation — formed by treaties between nations (e.g. UNEP) |
| GO | Governmental Organisation — national body with legislative power (e.g. EPA) |
| NGO | Non-Governmental Organisation — independent, publicly funded (e.g. Greenpeace) |
| Diplomatic constraint | Political limitations on action due to the need for international consensus |
| Watchdog | An organisation that monitors and publicly reports on violations without legal authority |
